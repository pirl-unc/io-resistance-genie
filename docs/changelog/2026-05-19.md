# Changelog — 2026-05-19

Two novel emerging findings added to the TME exclusion class.

## Novel

### P2RX7 in Tregs drives Treg accumulation and CTLA-4-mediated immunosuppression in lung cancer

- **Classification:** novel
- **Paper:** Santiago-Carvalho I et al. "Regulatory T-cell Sensing of Extracellular ATP via P2RX7 Promotes Their Accumulation and Suppression and Drives Lung Tumor Growth." *Cancer Immunology Research* 14(4):658–675, April 2026. [DOI: 10.1158/2326-6066.CIR-25-0567](https://aacrjournals.org/cancerimmunolres/article/14/4/658/776524/)
- **What it adds:** P2RX7, an extracellular ATP-gated purinergic receptor, is expressed at elevated levels on tumor-infiltrating Tregs in both mouse and human lung cancer. P2RX7 signaling promotes Treg accumulation in the TME and upregulates CTLA-4, reinforcing Treg suppressive capacity. T cell-specific P2RX7 knockout or pharmacological inhibition reduces Treg infiltration, increases CD4+ effector T cells, and improves tumor control in mouse models. Higher P2RX7 expression correlates with worse survival in human lung cancer patients. The mechanism is conceptually distinct from anti-CTLA-4 antibody (which depletes Tregs via FcR-mediated ADCC) and potentially complementary. It also connects tumor metabolic stress (extracellular ATP from dying cells) to adaptive immune suppression via a purinergic receptor — a mechanistic bridge not previously represented in the synthesis.

### CysLTR1 programs tumor-promoting myelopoiesis; montelukast restores ICI efficacy pan-cancer

- **Classification:** novel
- **Paper:** Zhang B et al. "Targeting cysteinyl leukotriene receptor 1 reprograms tumor-promoting myelopoiesis and overcomes immune checkpoint therapy resistance." *Nature Cancer*, May 19, 2026. [DOI: 10.1038/s43018-026-01174-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43018-026-01174-7)
- **What it adds:** Cysteinyl leukotriene receptor 1 (CysLTR1) programs bone marrow myeloid progenitors toward an immunosuppressive, tumor-promoting fate. Blocking CysLTR1 — genetically or with the FDA-approved asthma drug montelukast — does not deplete these myeloid cells but reprograms them into immunostimulatory effectors. In five syngeneic mouse tumor models (TNBC, melanoma, ovarian, colon, prostate), CysLTR1 blockade combined with anti-PD-1 slowed tumor growth, improved survival, and restored ICI responsiveness even in tumors that had acquired resistance to checkpoint therapy. Findings were validated in human immune cells, human tumor tissue, and large patient cancer datasets. The mechanism differs from prior myeloid-targeting strategies (CXCR2 blockade, CSF1R blockade) that deplete cells rather than re-educating them. Montelukast's established clinical safety profile makes this directly actionable for rapid ICI combination trials.
