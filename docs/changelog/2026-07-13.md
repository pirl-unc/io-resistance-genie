## Novel findings

### 1. Gut microbiome drives MHC-II restoration to overcome ICI resistance in MSS CRC

- **Classification:** novel
- **Paper:** bioRxiv (2026-06-30) — "Microbial induction of MHC-II expression in colon cancer cells overcomes immunotherapy resistance and limits metastasis"
- **DOI:** [10.64898/2026.06.30.735621](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.30.735621)
- **What it adds:** Loss of epithelial MHC-II expression is a driver of immune evasion in intestinal tumors; specific gut microbial signals can restore MHC-II antigen presentation capacity in MSS CRC cells, promoting T cell recognition and overcoming PD-1 blockade resistance. This connects the gut microbiome to antigen presentation — two independently implicated resistance axes — via a direct epithelial mechanism. MSS CRC is the canonical ICI-refractory indication, making this a high-relevance target finding.

### 2. Neutrophil state heterogeneity and ICAM1 as ICI response biomarker in HNSCC

- **Classification:** novel
- **Paper:** bioRxiv (2026-07-02) — "Single-cell multi-omics analysis reveals heterogeneity and plasticity of neutrophil states in response to immunotherapies"
- **DOI:** [10.64898/2026.07.02.735691](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.02.735691)
- **Authors:** Gao, Shyamkumar, Winn, Erbe, Davis, Zaborek, Heimstreet, Boyenga, Matthews, Tsao, Sondel, Dinh
- **What it adds:** Single-cell multi-omics in HNSCC maps neutrophil state diversity; neutrophil-activating therapy (NAT: anti-CD40 + TNFα + Cetuximab) induces anti-tumoral states absent from cold tumors; ICAM1 expression on neutrophils marks ICI-responsive states and functions as a response biomarker; elevated TAN density in cold tumors correlates with poor ICI prognosis. Extends the myeloid heterogeneity paradigm to neutrophil plasticity as a determinant of checkpoint sensitivity.
- **Added to:** `docs/mechanisms/tme-exclusion.md` (emerging), `docs/index.md` (emerging)

### 3. Paracrine STING→ferroptosis relay via ACSL4 lactylation synergizes with anti-PD-1 in CRC

- **Classification:** novel
- **Paper:** PNAS Volume 123, Issue 26 (2026-06-30) — "Immune cell–intrinsic STING activation drives tumor ferroptosis via AA-mediated suppression of ACSL4 lactylation in colorectal cancer"
- **DOI:** [10.1073/pnas.2524594123](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2524594123)
- **What it adds:** STING activation in immune cells drives paracrine ferroptosis of adjacent CRC cells: TBK1 phosphorylates cPLA2 at Ser505, releasing arachidonic acid (AA) into the TME; CRC cells take up AA, which inhibits EP300-mediated ACSL4 K426 lactylation and promotes ACSL4-dependent ferroptosis. STING agonist + anti-PD-1 synergize via this relay. This is mechanistically orthogonal to the existing IFN-γ→IRF1→AGPAT3 ferroptosis axis — it is immune compartment-initiated and paracrine, placing the ferroptosis-sensitizing signal outside the tumor cell. ACSL4 K426 lactylation is a newly identified PTM node.
- **Added to:** `docs/mechanisms/metabolic.md` (emerging), `docs/index.md` (emerging)
