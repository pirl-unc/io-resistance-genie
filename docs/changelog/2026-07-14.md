## Novel findings

### 1. Diet–microbiome synergy is the mechanistic basis for the obesity paradox in ICI response

- **Classification:** novel
- **Paper:** Nature (2026-07-08) — "Diet–microbiome synergy underlies obesity-associated immunotherapy efficacy"
- **DOI:** [10.1038/s41586-026-10750-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10750-x)
- **Authors:** Lysanne Desharnais, Anikka Swaby, Meriem Messaoudene, Samuel Doré, et al.
- **What it adds:** The obesity paradox in ICI (high BMI → better anti-PD-1 response) is mechanistically explained by diet-driven gut microbial ecosystem features rather than by adiposity or metabolic dysfunction per se. Across 12 custom mouse diet models, ICI sensitivity tracks with gut microbial state, not body weight. An obesogenic diet promotes a robust and persistent gut microbial ecosystem that enhances anti-PD-1 efficacy; short-term dietary switching or human-to-mouse FMT restores ICI sensitivity — decoupling the benefit from bodyweight itself. This upgrades diet from an observational correlate (high-fiber → better outcomes) to a mechanistically tractable intervention target, and identifies dietary modification as complementary to FMT as an ICI-sensitization strategy. All experiments preclinical (mouse); no direct human clinical trial data.
