### P2RY2: adaptive ICI resistance via tumor-cell eATP sensing and PGE2 amplification
- **Paper:** Hu et al. 2026, *Immunity* (2026-07-02). DOI: [10.1016/j.immuni.2026.06.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.immuni.2026.06.006)
- **Finding:** Immunotherapy-induced extracellular ATP (eATP) — released from dying tumor and immune cells — signals through P2RY2 on surviving tumor cells, upregulating COX1/2 and accumulating PGE2 to re-establish TME immunosuppression. This feedback circuit amplifies with each round of ICI-mediated killing, constituting adaptive resistance. Genetic deletion or pharmacological P2RY2 inhibition reduces baseline intratumoral PGE2, remodels the immune infiltrate, and reverses resistance to four distinct immunotherapy modalities (CAR-T, TCR-T, checkpoint blockade, TIL) across CRC, pancreatic, and melanoma preclinical models. An anti-P2RY2 mAb enhanced melanoma patients' immune cells' ability to kill their own tumor cells ex vivo. P2RY2 acts upstream of COX/PGE2 as the on-treatment eATP sensor — mechanistically distinct from both B2M/JAK LOF and from broad COX2 inhibition strategies.
- **Triage:** relevance 5/5, novelty 5/5

### GABA–GABBR–gMDSC axis: sex-specific immunosuppression in GBM
- **Paper:** Pathak & Deneen 2026, *Nature Cancer* (2026-06-23). DOI: [10.1038/s43018-026-01183-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43018-026-01183-6)
- **Finding:** Tumor-derived GABA activates GABBR (GABA receptor B) on granulocytic MDSCs, upregulating the CAT2–L-arginine–NOS2 axis to suppress antitumor T cell activity — but exclusively in female GBM preclinical models. GABBR agonism promotes GBM growth in female mice via gMDSC-mediated immunosuppression; pharmacological GABBR antagonism extends survival and reduces NOS2 in tumor-infiltrating gMDSCs in female but not male mice. A classical inhibitory neurotransmitter, produced by the tumor, is co-opted to drive myeloid immunosuppression in a sex-hormone-dependent manner. Directly implies that GBM ICI trials should incorporate sex-stratified analyses to detect potential GABBR-antagonism combination benefits.
- **Triage:** relevance 3/5, novelty 4/5

### Citraconate depletion drives exhausted CD8 T cell ferroptosis via PDE1A/C–cAMP–PKA–ALOX5
- **Paper:** Liu et al. 2026, *Science Immunology* (2026-05-01). DOI: [10.1126/sciimmunol.adz0348](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciimmunol.adz0348)
- **Finding:** Citraconate (an itaconate isomer) is markedly depleted in CD8 T cells subjected to chronic antigen stimulation or hypoxic conditions in the tumor microenvironment. Citraconate supplementation preserves stem-like T cell characteristics, attenuates ferroptosis, and potentiates anti-tumor immunity. Mechanistically, citraconate suppresses PDE1A/C expression to maintain cAMP, which activates PKA signaling, preserves mitochondrial integrity, and represses ALOX5 transcription to prevent arachidonic acid peroxidation. Lower ALOX5 or PDE1A expression in patients predicts less T cell exhaustion and better ICI responsiveness. This metabolite-depletion vulnerability is orthogonal to TOX/NFAT-mediated exhaustion programs, BETi/polyamine (ODC1) reinvigoration, and existing ferroptosis axes (TMEM87A-Golgi pH, IFN-γ–IRF1–AGPAT3), and provides a patient-level biomarker pair (ALOX5/PDE1A) ready for prospective ICI cohort validation.
- **Triage:** relevance 4/5, novelty 4/5
