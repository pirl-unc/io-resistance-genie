## Novel additions

### 1. Tumor-induced thymic central tolerance via CCR9-dependent pDC recruitment
**Citation:** Wang, Kato, Katakai 2026. *Science Advances*, 2026-07-23. DOI: [10.1126/sciadv.adx9864](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adx9864)

**Finding:** Tumor progression induces thymic accumulation of two distinct pDC populations via CCR9. CDP-pDCs migrate from the periphery to the thymus, where they present tumor antigens via MHC-I and MHC-II and drive clonal deletion of tumor-reactive developing T cells. Concurrently, CLP-pDCs secrete IFN-α to suppress thymopoiesis and reduce naïve T cell output. CCR9 deficiency abrogates both populations, enhancing antitumor immunity and reducing tumor growth in syngeneic mouse models.

**Why novel:** A systemic immune evasion axis entirely outside the TME — operating at the level of thymic central tolerance and T cell repertoire selection. Mechanistically orthogonal to all known TME-based resistance nodes (PD-L1/checkpoint ligands, MDSCs, Tregs, TGF-β exclusion). If operative in humans, implies that checkpoint blockade cannot rescue T cells that were never generated. Added to `docs/index.md` (emerging) and `docs/mechanisms/antigen-presentation.md` (emerging).

**Category:** `antigen_presentation`, `tme_exclusion`

### 2. Mutant TP53 retains mTORC1-suppressive function to drive autophagy-mediated ICI resistance in CRC
**Citation:** Labani-Motlagh & Lu 2026. *Cell Death and Disease*, 2026-07-22. DOI: [10.1038/s41419-026-09067-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41419-026-09067-4)

**Finding:** Certain TP53 mutations (G242V, S258I) in CRC selectively retain the ability to suppress mTORC1 signaling despite losing canonical p21-induction activity. Sustained mTORC1 inhibition drives elevated autophagy that protects tumor cells from IFN-γ- and TNF-α-induced apoptosis under immune-mediated stress, conferring anti-PD-1 resistance. CRISPR deletion of mutant Trp53 in MC38 mouse CRC cells restores mTORC1, reduces autophagy, markedly increases CD8+ TIL infiltration and clonal expansion, and reduces Tregs. Human p53 mutants regulate the same mTORC1 inhibitor gene sets in human CRC cell lines. Pharmacological mTOR activation or autophagy inhibition sensitizes mutant-p53 CRC tumors to ICI.

**Why novel:** Inverts the standard expectation that TP53 LOF is purely a growth disinhibitor — certain mutants actively confer immune evasion via retained mTORC1 suppression → autophagy → resistance to cytokine-mediated apoptosis. Identifies a tractable vulnerability (mTOR activation or autophagy blockade) for ICI combination in the most common mutation in MSS CRC. Note: MC38 is MSI-H; generalizability to MSS CRC requires further study. Added to `docs/index.md` (emerging) and `docs/mechanisms/tumor-intrinsic.md` (emerging).

**Category:** `tumor_intrinsic_signaling`, `metabolic`
