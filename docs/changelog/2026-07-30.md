## Novel additions

### 1. MEK-dependent bioenergetic demand drives terminal CD8+ T cell exhaustion
**Citation:** Mitra T, Park A, Vardhana SA 2026. *Immunity*, 2026-07-13. DOI: [10.1016/j.immuni.2026.06.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.immuni.2026.06.012). PMID: [42442358](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42442358/)

**Finding:** Chronic TCR engagement increases cellular ATP demand through MEK-dependent elevation of global protein synthesis rates in vivo and in vitro. This bioenergetic overload leads to mitochondrial NADH accumulation, ROS production, and progressive mitochondrial dysfunction characteristic of terminal exhaustion. Among TCR-proximal signaling components, MEK inhibition uniquely reduces nutrient uptake and mitochondrial NADH accumulation while restoring proliferation. MEK inhibition reverses chronic stimulation-driven RNA Pol II CTD phosphorylation, selectively reducing transcription at exhaustion-associated loci while maintaining memory-associated transcription. MEK inhibition attenuates terminal T cell exhaustion both in vitro and in vivo.

**Why novel:** Reframes terminal exhaustion progression as partly driven by an upstream bioenergetic overload — a metabolic demand mechanism upstream of the TOX/chromatin-fixation circuitry. Orthogonal to the polyamine (BETi/ODC1) and citraconate exhaustion-metabolic axes recently identified. FDA-approved MEK inhibitors make near-term clinical testing feasible without new drug development. Added to `docs/index.md` (emerging) and `docs/mechanisms/t-cell-exhaustion.md` (emerging).

**Category:** `t_cell_exhaustion`, `metabolic`

### 2. CARM1 epigenetic enzyme selectively suppresses cDC1 cross-presentation in tumors
**Citation:** Zhang X, Wucherpfennig KW et al. 2026. *Science*, 2026-07-09. DOI: [10.1126/science.aea1200](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aea1200). PMID: [42424445](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42424445/)

**Finding:** CARM1 (coactivator-associated arginine methyltransferase 1) is a selective negative regulator of cancer antigen cross-presentation by cDC1s, but not cDC2s. Carm1 inactivation promotes cDC1 antigen cross-presentation, activation, and accumulation in tumors. Pharmacological CARM1 inhibition enhances cDC1-mediated T cell priming in the context of a cancer neoantigen vaccine. Mechanistically, CARM1 inhibition increases chromatin accessibility at BATF3-Jun and RelA binding sites critical for cDC1 function. TGF-β regulates Carm1 expression, linking tumor-derived immunosuppressive signals to epigenetic repression of cDC1. Validated in both mouse and human settings.

**Why novel:** Identifies a DC-intrinsic epigenetic resistance axis entirely separate from stromal exclusion. Reveals a parallel, pharmacologically tractable mechanism by which TGF-β silences tumor immunity — DC-intrinsic epigenetic repression of antigen presentation — independent of the TGF-β fibroblast/exclusion biology already documented. Added to `docs/index.md` (emerging) and `docs/mechanisms/antigen-presentation.md` (emerging).

**Category:** `antigen_presentation`, `tme_exclusion`
