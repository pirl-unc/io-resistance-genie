# Changelog — 2026-09-05

## 1 novel finding added

**[tme_exclusion]** TIME subtype transitions during ICI therapy — not baseline TME classification — are the stronger predictor of response and survival across 10 cancer types (Cancer Cell 2026)[^doi:10.1016/j.ccell.2026.08.007].

A pan-cancer longitudinal scRNA-seq atlas (241 patients, 441 samples, 10 cancer types) defines 4 conserved TIME subtypes (inflamed, myeloid-dominant, B-cell-enriched, excluded) and shows that ~40% of tumors shift subtype during ICI therapy. Critically, the direction of this transition — not baseline subtype — is more predictive of objective response and overall survival. Favorable transitions toward inflamed or B-cell-enriched subtypes associate with response; persistence in or shift toward myeloid-dominant subtype is the acquired-resistance endpoint. CCR7+ dendritic cell recruitment and T cell reinvigoration hallmark favorable remodeling.

This extends the inflamed/excluded/desert taxonomy — anchored by Tumeh 2014 and Chen & Mellman 2017 — from a static pre-treatment classifier to a dynamic, on-treatment monitoring framework. Myeloid-dominant TIME lock-in emerges as a tractable acquired-resistance endpoint for future combination strategies.

**Classification:** novel — no prior claim in the synthesis addresses TIME state transitions or longitudinal TME trajectory as a predictive variable.
