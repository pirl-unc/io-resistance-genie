## Novel

### PLA2G2D: pre-TME secretory phospholipase checkpoint in tumour-draining lymph nodes

**Paper:** van Krimpen A et al. "PLA2G2D in tumour-draining lymph nodes regulates anti-tumour immunity." *Nature* (2026-09-09). DOI: [10.1038/s41586-026-10954-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10954-1)

**Classification:** `tme_exclusion` | `emerging` | mouse (primary data) + human (correlative)

**Key finding:** PLA2G2D, a secretory phospholipase expressed by macrophages in the paracortex of tumour-draining lymph nodes (TDLNs), suppresses early CD8+ T cell proliferation via an enzyme-independent mechanism. PLA2G2D and PD-1 are non-redundant checkpoints at distinct anatomical sites. Antibody blockade (AI-306) restores anti-tumour immunity in anti-PD-1-resistant preclinical models; combination is additive/synergistic. Validated in human melanoma TDLNs by spatial transcriptomics (Erasmus MC) and in >9,000 tumours computationally (Apeximmune).

**Why novel:** No prior claim in the synthesis addresses a TDLN-resident phospholipase as an immune checkpoint. The pre-trafficking anatomical site means PD-1 resistance mechanisms in the TME (exhaustion, PD-L1 upregulation, antigen loss) are orthogonal — combination treatment would target independent bottlenecks. Identifies the first enzyme-independent phospholipase immune suppression mechanism.
