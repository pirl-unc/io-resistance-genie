    Tumor microenvironment exclusion¶

    Confidently known¶

    • human Pre-existing CD8+ T-cell infiltrate at the invasive margin predicts pembrolizumab response in melanoma (Tumeh 2014)1. The inflamed / excluded / desert taxonomy has been validated pan-tumor and anchors the cancer-immunity set-point framework (Chen & Mellman 2017)2.
    • human mouse TGF-β signaling in peritumoral fibroblasts excludes T cells. Mariathasan 2018 in urothelial carcinoma (IMvigor210) defined the F-TBRS (fibroblast TGF-β response signature) in atezolizumab non-responders, whose T cells were trapped in collagen-rich stroma3. Tauriello 2018 established the mouse mechanism in a quadruple-mutant MSS CRC GEMM where TGF-β blockade unleashes Th1/CTL responses and renders liver metastases susceptible to anti-PD-L14.

    Contradictions / surprises¶

    • human TGF-β is biologically robust but has clinically disappointed. Bintrafusp alfa (TGFβRII trap / anti-PD-L1 bifunctional) failed pivotal trials — INTR@PID lung 037 discontinued 2021, biliary tract halted. Galunisertib + durvalumab, NIS793 combinations in MSS CRC — modest or negative. Likely reflects redundancy (TGF-β has pleiotropic roles), dose-limiting cardiotoxicity, or context-specific effects. A textbook translational gap between compelling biology and clinical benefit.
    • human IPRES underperformed as an independent predictor. Hugo 20165 nominated an EMT/wound-healing/angiogenesis transcriptomic signature in innately resistant melanoma. Subsequent validation across independent cohorts has been mixed. IPRES overlaps heavily with TGF-β and stromal signatures and adds little beyond CD8, PD-L1, TMB, and pan-fibroblast measures.
    • mouse WNT/β-catenin activation as exclusion driver — stronger in mice than in patients. Spranger 2015 defined the CCL4 / Batf3+ DC recruitment mechanism6. Clinical correlations weaker; WNT-inhibitor + ICI combinations limited by toxicity.

    Suspected but unconfirmed¶

    • mouse TROP2 associates with claudin-7 to regulate tight junctions and exclude T cells from TNBC7. TROP2 loss or hRS7 (sacituzumab govitecan antibody component) targeting restores infiltration and enhances anti-PD-1 efficacy. Reframes TROP2 from pure ADC target to a barrier-mechanism target. Single humanized TROP2 syngeneic model; needs broader preclinical and patient validation.
    • human hMENA-high TGF-β-driven CAFs define an ICT-resistance transcriptional program validated against OAK phase III in NSCLC (this period)8. Signature-level clinical anchoring but mechanistic causality is inferential from in vitro CAF experiments.

    Emerging¶

    • human Single-cell spatial transcriptomics defines six tissue niches that stratify neoadjuvant PD-1/PD-L1 response in cSCC better than PD-L1 IHC9. High antigen-presentation / B-plasma / inflammatory-keratinocyte niches enrich responders; proliferative-keratinocyte / low-APC-myeloid / fibroblast-rich-EMT niches dominate non-responders. Niche-based biomarkers may eventually outperform single-marker IHC.
    • human LOAd703 (CD40L/4-1BBL oncolytic adenovirus) + atezolizumab in anti-PD-1-refractory melanoma reprograms the myeloid compartment and restores ICI-responsive immune signatures in 24 patients10. Biomarker-level evidence; small single-arm; but few interventions reliably rescue refractory melanoma.
    • mouse TREM2+ immunosuppressive macrophages are spatially enriched in T cell-excluded CRC niches and co-define ICI resistance with multi-checkpoint T cell exhaustion11. Quadruple blockade (PD-1 + CTLA-4 + LAG-3 + anti-TREM2) achieves 100% clearance in dMMR CRC and >70% in MSS CRC orthotopic models; spatial co-localization of CXCL9+ macrophages with TCF+PRF1+ T cells predicts anti-PD-1 response. First evidence that TREM2-mediated myeloid exclusion is co-tractable with conventional checkpoints in MSS CRC.
    • human Pan-cancer spatial ecotype (SE) taxonomy (9 SEs from 10 M+ cells) recoverable non-invasively from plasma cfDNA (Liquid EcoTyper), with cfDNA SE levels in ~100 melanoma patients showing striking ICI-response associations12. Establishes that spatially distinct TME exclusion/infiltration states can be inferred from blood; opens non-invasive monitoring of TME configuration as a resistance biomarker.
    • mouse STING agonists paradoxically expand immunosuppressive regulatory B cells (Bregs) via PI3Kγ in pancreatic cancer, partially offsetting STING-driven myeloid activation13. Dual STING agonist + PI3Kγ inhibition (SH-273/Nano-273) eliminates this Breg escape while preserving IRF3-driven myeloid activation, extending median survival to 201 days in orthotopic PDAC models. Identifies regulatory B cells as a TME exclusion-class resistance mechanism and explains why STING monotherapy underperforms in an immunosuppressive TME.
    • human mouse P2RX7 in tumor-infiltrating Tregs senses extracellular ATP in the TME, promoting Treg accumulation and CTLA-4-mediated immunosuppression in lung cancer14. T cell-specific P2RX7 deletion reduces Treg infiltration, increases CD4+ effector T cell accumulation, and improves tumor control; pharmacological P2RX7 inhibition phenocopies this. Elevated P2RX7 in tumor Tregs correlates with worse survival in human lung cancer (Santiago-Carvalho 2026, Cancer Immunol Res). Connects tumor-derived ATP — a metabolic danger signal — to Treg persistence via a purinergic receptor, an unexpected bridge between metabolic stress in the TME and adaptive immune suppression that is mechanistically distinct from anti-CTLA-4 antibody.

    • human mouse CysLTR1 programs tumor-promoting myelopoiesis that suppresses antitumor immunity; blockade with montelukast (an FDA-approved asthma drug) reprograms these cells and restores ICI efficacy15. Blocking CysLTR1 converts immunosuppressive tumor-associated neutrophils and myeloid cells into immunostimulatory phenotypes — not depletion but re-education — and in five syngeneic mouse cancer models (TNBC, melanoma, ovarian, colon, prostate) restored anti-PD-1 efficacy including in tumors with acquired ICI resistance (Zhang 2026, Nature Cancer). Validated in human immune cells, tumor tissue, and patient datasets. Montelukast's established safety profile positions CysLTR1 blockade as a rapidly translatable ICI combination partner.

    • human mouse Cancer-induced nerve injury (CINI) promotes anti-PD-1 resistance via IL-6/IFN-β neuronal signalling that remodels the TME16. Perineural invasion burden correlates with worse ICI outcomes in patients; anti-IL-6R (tocilizumab) rescues anti-PD-1 sensitivity in nerve-injured tumour models. Connects peripheral nervous system remodelling — a common cancer hallmark — to T-cell exclusion and immune evasion.

    • human SpaceCat spatial multiplex imaging of 103 TNBC patients in the TONIC phase 2 trial shows spatial diversity and T-cell border infiltration outperform bulk PD-L1 IHC for predicting ICI response17. Spatial context of immune cell positioning, not type abundance, predicts outcome; the publicly available SpaceCat framework enables systematic spatial TME classification.
    • mouse GIV/Girdin endocytic adaptor controls PD-1 surface availability on tumour macrophages; GIV loss causes hyperprogressive disease under anti-PD-118. Psychotropic drugs that disrupt PD-1 endocytosis phenocopy GIV loss. Defines a macrophage-intrinsic endocytic checkpoint as a distinct resistance axis.
    • human Mesenchymal (MES) GBM transcriptional subtype predicts improved ICB survival in 181 patients (Ghannam 2026)19. MES GBM is marked by high HLA-I expression and greater T cell infiltration; transition from MES to non-mesenchymal state is the molecular correlate of acquired ICB resistance. The paradox — mesenchymal GBM is generally more aggressive — is explained by unexpectedly higher antigen presentation capacity and immune accessibility in this transcriptional state.
    • human Single-cell multi-omics reveals heterogeneous and plastic neutrophil states that determine ICI outcomes in HNSCC20. Neutrophil-activating therapy (NAT: anti-CD40 + TNFα + Cetuximab) induces distinct anti-tumoral neutrophil states absent from cold tumors; ICAM1 expression marks ICI-responsive neutrophil states and functions as a biomarker of ICI response; elevated TAN density in cold tumors correlates with poor ICI prognosis (Gao et al. 2026). Extends the myeloid heterogeneity paradigm to neutrophil state plasticity as a direct determinant of checkpoint sensitivity.
    • mouse human PID1 maintains immunosuppressive TAM identity by limiting oxysterol production; PID1 loss in myeloid cells generates oxysterols that switch macrophage fate from immunosuppressive to antitumoral phenotypes, promoting T cell infiltration and improving anti-PD-1 efficacy21. PID1 deficiency upregulates LDL receptor → oxysterol accumulation → macrophage reprogramming. Pan-cancer human TAM expression data confirm elevated PID1 with immunosuppressive gene signatures; mouse genetic loss-of-function establishes the causal role. A new lipid metabolic axis controlling TAM fate determination, orthogonal to existing strategies (CysLTR1 blockade, TREM2 depletion, CSF1R inhibition). See also metabolic.
    • mouse Tumor-derived GABA activates GABBR on granulocytic MDSCs to drive CAT2–L-arginine–NOS2 T cell suppression in GBM, exclusively in female mice22. GABBR agonism promotes GBM growth through gMDSC-mediated immunosuppression in female preclinical models; pharmacological GABBR antagonism extends survival and reduces NOS2 in tumor-infiltrating gMDSCs in female but not male mice. A sex-specific neurotransmitter–myeloid immunosuppressive axis: GBM-produced GABA co-opts a classical inhibitory neurotransmitter to suppress antitumor T cell activity in a sex-hormone-dependent manner, via myeloid cells rather than neurons. GBM ICI trials stratifying by sex may reveal a GABBR-antagonism combination benefit limited to female patients — a rare mechanistic sex distinction in solid tumour immunotherapy (Pathak & Deneen 2026, Nature Cancer).
    • human TIME subtype transitions during ICI therapy — not baseline TME classification — are the stronger predictor of response and survival, pan-cancer23. A longitudinal scRNA-seq atlas (241 patients, 441 samples, 10 cancer types) defines 4 conserved TIME subtypes (inflamed, myeloid-dominant, B-cell-enriched, excluded); ~40% of tumors shift subtype during therapy. Favorable transitions toward inflamed or B-cell-enriched subtypes associate with response and improved OS; persistence in or shift toward the myeloid-dominant subtype is the acquired-resistance endpoint. CCR7+ DC recruitment and T cell reinvigoration are hallmarks of favorable remodeling. Reframes TIME biomarker strategy from a static pre-treatment snapshot to a dynamic longitudinal monitoring framework, and directly extends the inflamed/excluded/desert classification from a baseline predictor to an on-treatment resistance determinant.

    Practical takeaways¶

    • Inflamed vs. excluded vs. desert remains a useful pre-treatment mental model. A patient with high CD8 at the invasive margin but non-response may have a downstream exhaustion or metabolic problem; a patient with a desert tumor faces a different biology (likely WNT/β-catenin, STK11, low TMB, or low DC recruitment).
    • Do not count on TGF-β-targeting agents to rescue excluded tumors yet. The clinical data on bintrafusp alfa, galunisertib, NIS793 are disappointing despite strong preclinical rationale.
    • CysLTR1/montelukast — an FDA-approved asthma drug that blocks CysLTR1 reprograms tumor-promoting myeloid cells rather than depleting them, restoring ICI response pan-cancer in preclinical models. Rapid clinical deployment as an ICI combination partner is conceivable given the established safety data; combination trials are anticipated.
    • TIME subtype at baseline is necessary but not sufficient — a new pan-cancer atlas (n=241) shows that the trajectory of change during therapy matters more than where you start. A patient transitioning to myeloid-dominant during treatment has an adverse acquired resistance endpoint that is now measurable. This should motivate longitudinal biopsy strategies and on-treatment TME monitoring in ICI combination trials.
